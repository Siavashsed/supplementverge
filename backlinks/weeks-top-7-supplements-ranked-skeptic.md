## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is creatine still the best-supported supplement, and is berberine really a natural alternative to GLP-1 drugs?

Short answer: yes to the first, and no to the second.

Creatine monohydrate remains the best-supported performance supplement we have. Hundreds of trials show it improves strength, power output and lean mass when combined with resistance training. There is also growing evidence for benefits in cognition, especially under sleep deprivation or in older adults. The usual dose is 3 to 5 grams a day, taken consistently. You don't need a loading phase, and you don't need an expensive branded form. Plain monohydrate is the one with the research behind it. Its safety record in healthy people is excellent.

Berberine is a different story. It has some real data behind it, mainly modest effects on fasting glucose and lipids in people with metabolic issues. The trials are generally small, short and variable in quality, and the doses used were typically around 900 to 1,500 mg a day, split across meals. It can also cause gastrointestinal side effects and interacts with some medications. Calling it 'nature's GLP-1' is marketing. GLP-1 drugs produce large, well-documented weight loss in big randomized trials. Berberine does not come close to that.

The practical problem is that many 'metabolic stacks' include berberine at a token dose, far below anything studied, so even the modest research effect doesn't apply. Always check the dose per serving and compare it with the studies. Be wary of proprietary blends that hide amounts.
