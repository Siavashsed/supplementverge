## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Do collagen peptides actually help tendons and joints, or is it just marketing?

Short answer: they probably help a little, in specific people, at specific doses. They don't do what most product labels imply.

I'm a sports scientist and registered nutritionist, so here is how I think about it.

What the evidence supports: collagen peptides taken with vitamin C shortly before loading may give tendons more of the amino acids they use to build and repair tissue. That makes the most sense for people who are already training the tendon with progressive resistance work, and especially for those with a chronic problem such as Achilles pain. Typical doses in the research are around 15 g, taken roughly 45 to 60 minutes before the session.

What it doesn't support: the idea that a tub of powder will rebuild your knee cartilage. Eating collagen doesn't send it to the joint you want it in, and the claims about cartilage regeneration run well ahead of the data.

My own experience matches this. In 2022 I ran a small informal trial with eleven recreational runners using 15 g of hydrolysed collagen plus vitamin C about 50 minutes before tendon sessions. Over ten weeks, the only change anyone could measure was in the two runners with chronic Achilles pain. It was a small sample and not proof of anything, but it fits the broader picture.

Practical takeaways:

1. Collagen is an adjunct to loading and rehab, never a replacement.
2. Timing and vitamin C seem to matter.
3. If you're healthy with no tendon issues, expect little.
4. If you have persistent pain, see a physio or doctor before spending money on supplements.
