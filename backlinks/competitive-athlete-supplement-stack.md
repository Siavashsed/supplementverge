## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How many supplements does a competitive athlete actually need?

Fewer than almost everyone assumes. For most competitive athletes the evidence-backed list is about four items, occasionally five, and the rest of what's in the cabinet is doing very little.

I'm a sports scientist and registered nutritionist, and a few years ago I ran an informal audit with a 14-person university rowing squad. We emptied everyone's lockers onto a gym floor and counted 63 products. The athletes estimated they were spending around $310 a month each. When I went through the research behind each product, only three had a trial I'd call convincing.

Here is how I'd approach the question.

1. Start with the basics. Total energy intake, protein spread sensibly across the day, carbohydrate matched to training load, hydration and sleep account for far more of the result than any capsule.

2. Look for human trials in trained people. A mechanism that works in a cell culture or in untrained volunteers doesn't tell you much about an athlete already close to their ceiling.

3. Check the dose. Many products contain a real ingredient at a fraction of the amount used in the studies, or hide the amount inside a proprietary blend.

4. Check for third-party testing. For anyone subject to drug testing, contamination is a genuine risk, and a cheap, untested product is not worth a ban.

5. Add one thing at a time. If you change five variables at once you won't know what worked, and you won't know what caused a problem.

The short answer is that if your stack is longer than your training log is consistent, it's probably the wrong way round.
