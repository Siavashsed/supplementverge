## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Did vitamin D actually help prevent or treat COVID-19?

Short answer: not in any way that justified the 2020 hype. Vitamin D is genuinely important, but the claim that it prevents or treats COVID-19 was never established.

Here is how the story took off. Early in the pandemic, several observational studies found that people hospitalised with COVID-19 tended to have lower vitamin D levels. That was easy to turn into a headline: cheap supplement, big protective effect. The problem is that low vitamin D is also common in older adults, people with obesity, people with chronic disease, and those who spend little time outdoors. All of those are independent risk factors for severe COVID-19. So low vitamin D may simply have been a marker of poorer baseline health, and in sick patients it can also fall as a result of inflammation. Observational data can't untangle that.

The proper test is a randomised controlled trial, and those arrived over the following months. Overall they did not show that vitamin D supplementation reliably reduced infection, hospitalisation or death from COVID-19. Large single doses given to people already ill did not change outcomes in the better-designed trials.

That does not mean you should ignore vitamin D. If you are deficient, particularly through winter, with darker skin, or with limited sun exposure, correcting it is sensible for bone and muscle health, and a normal-dose supplement is reasonable. Testing first is better than guessing. What the evidence does not support is megadosing, such as 10,000 IU daily across a whole team, as protection against a virus. Very high intakes over long periods carry their own risks, including raised calcium.
