## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is creatine monohydrate still the best supplement to take, or are newer products worth the money?

For most people, creatine monohydrate is still the best-value supplement available, and I say that as someone who gets sent new products to review almost every week.

The case rests on evidence. Creatine monohydrate has been studied for decades in trials covering strength, power output, lean mass gain and recovery. The typical dose of 3 to 5 grams a day is well established, and in healthy adults its safety record is strong. There is also growing research on cognition, particularly under sleep deprivation, and on older adults preserving muscle. And it's cheap: a two-month tub runs about $25.

Newer products are a different story. Many launch with a proprietary blend, a mechanism that sounds convincing, and perhaps one small trial funded by the manufacturer. That doesn't mean they're useless, but it does mean the evidence is thin and the price per serving is often many times higher.

Here's the filter I use before spending money on anything new:

1. What does it do that creatine, protein, caffeine, vitamin D or omega-3 doesn't already do?
2. Is the dose on the label the dose used in the studies?
3. Has anyone independent replicated the result, in humans, at a meaningful sample size?
4. Is the product third-party tested for what's actually in the tub?

If a product can't answer those, I'd rather you spend the money on food, sleep or a coach.

There are exceptions, and I'm happy to be proven wrong when good data arrives. But so far, very few launches clear that bar. I wrote up this week's top 7 with that lens, if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/supplementverge/weeks-top-7-supplements-november-2026/
