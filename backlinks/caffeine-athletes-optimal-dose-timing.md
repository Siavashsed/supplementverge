**Title:** Is a 300 mg pre-workout scoop doing anything that 3 mg/kg of caffeine wouldn't?

Something I wish I'd understood years ago: most of the research showing a performance benefit from caffeine uses about 3 mg/kg, not the 300-400 mg scoops that pre-workout labels advertise. For a 75 kg person that's roughly 225 mg. Studies generally find benefits from about 3 to 6 mg/kg taken around an hour beforehand, and the higher end doesn't reliably add more. It does add jitters, a higher heart rate and worse sleep.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best caffeine dose for athletes, and when should you take it before training?

For most athletes, the best-supported dose is about 3 mg/kg of bodyweight, taken roughly 60 minutes before you start. For a 70 kg person that's around 210 mg, which is about two small coffees. The research range runs from about 3 to 6 mg/kg, but the higher end tends to add side effects faster than it adds performance.

The timing matters because caffeine takes around 30 to 60 minutes to reach peak levels in the blood. Taking it as you walk into the gym means the peak arrives late. Taking it an hour early lines the peak up with your main work.

Here's why more isn't better. Past a moderate dose, the benefits level off, while jitters, a raised heart rate, stomach upset and anxiety keep climbing. I found this out in 2019, when I took a 400 mg pre-workout before a 5 am tempo session. My heart rate sat at 158 during what should have been an easy warm-up, my hands shook, and I had to bail on the session. That was well over the dose the evidence supports for my bodyweight.

A few practical rules:

1. Calculate your dose from bodyweight rather than relying on a scoop size.
2. Start at the low end, around 2 to 3 mg/kg, and only move up if you tolerate it well.
3. Test it in training, never for the first time on race day.
4. Mind the cutoff. Caffeine's half-life is around 5 hours, so an evening dose can disrupt sleep, and poor sleep undoes the benefit.
5. Habitual users may respond a little less, but the benefit usually doesn't vanish.
