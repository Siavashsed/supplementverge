## LINKEDIN
**Hook:** A vitamin D tablet bought on autopilot tells you nothing about your actual blood level.

Most people pick their vitamin D dose from the bottle, not from a blood test.

The dose-response research is fairly consistent: the same 1,000 IU a day produces very different results in different people. Starting level, body weight and absorption all change how far 25(OH)D actually moves. Someone starting deficient may rise meaningfully. Someone heavier, or already near range, may barely shift.

The fix is simple and cheap. Test before you start, choose a dose based on that number, take it with a meal that contains some fat, and retest after 8 to 12 weeks once levels have settled.

It is the same principle behind most health and performance work: measure, adjust, measure again. Guessing feels efficient until you find out a year later that nothing changed.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much vitamin D should I take per day, and is 1,000 IU enough?

It depends on your starting level, and that is the part most people skip.

For a typical adult, 1,000 IU (25 mcg) a day is a reasonable maintenance dose, and in the UK the NHS suggests at least 400 IU (10 mcg) daily through autumn and winter. But whether it is enough depends on your blood level of 25-hydroxyvitamin D, not the number on the label. A common reference point: below about 12 ng/mL (30 nmol/L) counts as deficient, and around 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) is adequate for most people, though some clinicians aim higher.

How far a dose moves you varies a lot. Very roughly, each extra 100 IU a day raises levels by about 0.5 to 1 ng/mL in many adults. The rise is bigger if you start low and smaller if you start near range or weigh more. Vitamin D is fat soluble, so taking it with a meal that contains some fat tends to help absorption.

The practical approach:

1. Get a 25(OH)D blood test before choosing a dose.
2. Pick a dose from that result, ideally with your doctor if you are low.
3. Take it with food.
4. Retest after 8 to 12 weeks, roughly how long levels take to settle.

Stay at or below 4,000 IU a day unless a doctor says otherwise. That is the adult upper limit in both UK/EU and US guidance, and long-term excess can raise blood calcium.
