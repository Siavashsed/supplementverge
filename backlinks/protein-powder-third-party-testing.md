## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if a protein powder has actually been third-party tested?

The honest answer is that the words on the front of the tub won't tell you. Phrases like 'lab tested', 'quality assured' or 'pharmaceutical grade' have no regulated meaning. They can describe a single test on one batch, an in-house check, or a report from some time ago that isn't tied to the tub you're holding.

What you want is a certification run by an independent body, and in practice two programs stand out: NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Sport. Both test product batch by batch, and both screen for substances banned in sport, not just for label accuracy. That is a different level of assurance from a brand commissioning its own test.

Here's how I'd check, in order:

1. Look for the actual logo on the tub, not just wording that sounds similar.
2. Verify it independently. Both NSF and Informed Sport maintain searchable online databases. Search by brand and product name, and ideally confirm the specific product, not just that the brand appears somewhere on the list.
3. Check the batch. Informed Sport in particular lets you look up batch numbers, which confirms the tub in your hand was the one tested.
4. Be wary of vague claims. If a company says 'independently tested' but won't name the lab or share results, treat that as a claim, not evidence.
