I work in sports science and ran a small informal test back in 2022 with a club cycling group in Leeds, 12 riders, before a 7 a.m. 40 km time trial. Half took a standard 300 mg pre-workout scoop. The other half dosed by body weight instead.

The scoop group didn't ride better. A couple of them had shaky hands and a racing heart on the start line, and one felt sick before the first 5 km. It's a small sample and not a controlled trial, so take it with salt. But it matches what the research keeps saying.

The best-supported range is 3 to 6 mg per kg of body weight, taken about 60 minutes before you perform. For a 70 kg rider that's roughly 210 to 420 mg, and most people do fine at the lower end. Past that you mostly buy jitters, not speed.

Two other things people skip: tolerance builds if you use it daily, so the same dose does less over time, and a flat 300 mg is a very different dose for a 55 kg person than for a 95 kg one.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much caffeine should an athlete take before a race or workout, and when?

The best-supported answer is 3 to 6 mg of caffeine per kilogram of body weight, taken roughly 60 minutes before you perform. For a 70 kg athlete that works out to about 210 to 420 mg. Most people get the benefit at the lower end, and going higher mostly adds side effects rather than performance.

The most common mistake I see is dosing by the product instead of by body weight. Many pre-workout scoops contain 300 mg or more, which is a modest dose for a heavy athlete and a very large one for a lighter athlete. In 2022 I tested a 12-person club cycling group before a 7 a.m. 40 km time trial in Leeds. Half took a 300 mg scoop. Several of those riders reported shaky hands and a racing heart at the start line, which is not where you want to be before hard effort. It was an informal test, not a controlled trial, but it lines up with the published research.

A few practical points:

1. Calculate your dose from your weight. Start at 3 mg/kg and only move up if you've tested it in training.
2. Take it about an hour before. Blood levels peak around then for most people.
3. Never try a new dose on race day. Test it in a normal session first so you know how your stomach and nerves respond.
4. Watch tolerance. If you use caffeine every day, the performance effect shrinks over time. Some athletes cut back for several days before key events to get more from it.
5. Mind the sleep cost. A late-day dose can wreck recovery, which defeats the purpose.
